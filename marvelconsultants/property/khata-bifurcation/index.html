Khata Bifurcation in Bangalore: Split One Khata for Co-owners, Heirs and Part Sales

Khata bifurcation in Bangalore is the process of splitting one khata into two or more separate khatas after a property has been legally divided, so that each owner gets their own khata and property tax record. It follows a registered partition, release or sale deed and does not create ownership by itself.

Who may need khata bifurcation?

Khata bifurcation may be relevant when a single property record needs to be separated to reflect a legally documented division. Common situations include:

  • Family members or co-owners dividing a jointly owned property.
  • Heirs arranging separate records after inheritance and a legally supported division.
  • Owners dividing property under a registered partition or release deed.
  • A seller and buyer dealing with a legally divided portion of a larger property.

The correct route depends on the property, ownership documents, local jurisdiction and applicable civic rules. A separate khata should not be assumed to be available simply because family members have agreed to divide a property.

What is khata bifurcation?

A khata is a civic property record used for property-tax assessment and related municipal purposes. Bifurcation means splitting one existing khata into separate records for portions that have been legally divided and are eligible to be recorded separately.

It is different from proving title. The registered deed and other relevant records establish the legal basis for the division; the khata process updates the civic record. Approval depends on the documents and the rules applicable to the property.

Documents required for khata bifurcation

The exact checklist varies with the property and the reason for division. Documents commonly requested may include:

  • The existing khata certificate, extract or relevant property record.
  • Recent property-tax receipts or payment records.
  • The registered partition, release, sale or other deed supporting the division.
  • An encumbrance certificate, where applicable.
  • A property sketch or plan showing the original property and proposed portions.
  • Identity and address proof of the relevant owners.
  • For inherited property, relevant death certificate and legal-heir or family documents, as applicable.
  • Any layout, land, building or approval records required for the particular property.

Do not assume that every item is mandatory in every case or that this list is exhaustive. The current official checklist for the property's jurisdiction should be confirmed before applying.

Khata bifurcation process in Bangalore

The steps below are a general guide. The applicable process may differ by property type, jurisdiction and current civic procedure.

Step 1: Review the existing property records

Collect the existing khata, tax records, ownership documents and relevant property details. Check whether the property and its current records are consistent.

Step 2: Confirm the legal basis for division

Identify the registered deed that supports the division. A private family understanding alone may not be sufficient for updating the civic record.

Step 3: Check the proposed portions and eligibility

Review the property sketch, relevant approvals and the requirements of the authority responsible for the property. Resolve material discrepancies before proceeding.

Step 4: Prepare the application documents

Arrange the required records and supporting documents in the format accepted by the relevant authority. Confirm whether an online or in-person route applies to the case.

Step 5: Submit through the applicable route

Follow the current official procedure for the property's jurisdiction. Keep the acknowledgement and copies of submitted documents.

Step 6: Respond to queries and check the updated records

If the authority raises objections or requests further documents, respond through the prescribed process. Once a decision is made, check the resulting khata and tax records for accuracy.

How long does khata bifurcation take, and what does it cost?

There is no single reliable timeline or fee that applies to every property. The process can depend on the jurisdiction, property category, document completeness, approval history, official charges and whether the authority raises objections.

Before starting, confirm the current official fees and any applicable professional-service charges in writing. Avoid relying on a guaranteed completion date or an unverified fee estimate.

Common problems that can delay or prevent bifurcation

  • Missing registered documents: The legal basis for dividing the property may not be adequately documented.
  • Mismatch in records: Names, property dimensions, addresses or tax details may differ across records.
  • Unclear property boundaries: The proposed portions may not be adequately shown in the sketch or supporting plan.
  • Approval or layout concerns: Certain properties may face restrictions related to required planning approvals or their existing status.
  • Wrong application route: The appropriate portal or office may depend on the property's location and category.
  • Incomplete inheritance records: Additional documents may be needed where the division follows a death or succession.

Frequently asked questions about khata bifurcation

1. What is khata bifurcation in Bangalore?

Khata bifurcation is the splitting of one khata record into two or more separate records after a property has been legally divided. Each owner then has their own khata and property tax record for their portion. It updates the civic record and does not by itself create or prove ownership.

2. What is the difference between khata bifurcation and khata transfer?

Khata transfer moves a khata from one owner to another, for example after a sale or a death. Khata bifurcation splits one khata into several. A property can need both: first the division and the bifurcation, and then a transfer when a portion is later sold.

3. Can I apply for khata bifurcation online?

Online availability depends on the property type, jurisdiction and current civic portal. Some khata services have moved online, but not every bifurcation case can be completed digitally. Check the official portal for the current route or ask us to review your case.

4. What documents are needed for khata bifurcation?

Usually the deed that divides the property (such as a registered partition, release, sale or gift deed), the existing khata, property tax receipts, an encumbrance certificate, a sketch showing the full property and each divided portion, and ID proof of the owners. For inherited property, the death certificate, legal heir documents and the family tree may also be needed. The exact list can change by case.

5. Is a private agreement or family arrangement enough for khata bifurcation?

No. A simple private agreement is not enough for the civic record. The division should be supported by a registered deed, such as a partition deed, a release deed or a sale deed, depending on how the property is being divided.

6. Can a B-Khata property, or a property without layout approval, be bifurcated?

Eligibility can be restricted, and some properties may face approval-related barriers. In January 2025, news reports described a BBMP direction concerning certain properties that lacked required BDA approval. B-Khata properties may also face limits on services. Have your documents reviewed and confirm the current rules with the responsible authority before applying.

7. Do I need a partition deed before khata bifurcation?

If co-owners or heirs are dividing a jointly owned property, a registered partition deed (or a registered release or family settlement, depending on the arrangement) is normally the basis for the bifurcation. If a portion has been sold, the registered sale deed is the basis. The right document depends on who is giving up what.

8. Do I need khata bifurcation before selling part of my property?

The required sequence depends on the transaction and property records. A separate khata may be needed for the portion being sold, but the exact legal and civic requirements should be confirmed before the sale. Plan the record update with the deed and transaction requirements in mind.
