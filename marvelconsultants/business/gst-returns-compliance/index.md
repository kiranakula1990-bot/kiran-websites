Most GST notices are born in a return: an ITC claim that GSTR-2B did not support, a GSTR-1 that did not match 3B, an annual return filed from memory. Compliance is a monthly discipline, not a year-end task.

Regular taxpayers file GSTR-1 (outward supplies) and GSTR-3B (summary return with tax payment) monthly — or quarterly under the QRMP scheme for smaller taxpayers — and the annual GSTR-9, with GSTR-9C reconciliation above the turnover limit. Input tax credit is now effectively limited to what appears in GSTR-2B, so reconciliation with suppliers is where compliance is won or lost.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: What is the QRMP scheme?**

A: Quarterly Return, Monthly Payment: taxpayers up to the prescribed turnover can file GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B quarterly while paying tax monthly through a simple challan. It reduces filings but not the need for monthly reconciliation.

**Q: Can I claim ITC on an invoice that is not in my GSTR-2B?**

A: As the law now stands, credit is restricted to what is reflected in GSTR-2B. If your supplier has not filed, the practical remedy is to get them to file and claim in the period it appears — not to claim it anyway and explain later.

**Q: We have not filed for several months. What happens?**

A: Late fees accrue per return, interest on unpaid tax, and after a continuous default the registration can be cancelled suo motu. Returns must be filed in sequence. We compute the exposure, file in order and, where appropriate, apply for revocation.
