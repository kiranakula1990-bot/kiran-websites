Most khata applications are delayed by paperwork, not by the portal. A name spelt two ways, a missing sketch or an unpaid tax year is enough to stop a file. This guide explains what khata documentation covers, gives a document checklist, and shows when to do it yourself and when to ask a consultant.

1. What is khata documentation?

Khata documentation is the set of records you need to create, update, transfer or split a khata. A khata is the civic record used for property tax and related municipal purposes. It does not prove title by itself, so the application depends on your deed and other records matching what the authority holds.

For a simple case, the set is the sale deed, an encumbrance certificate (EC), the latest tax receipt, your identity proof and, where relevant, a sketch. Mutation and bifurcation cases need more.

2. What do BBMP khata documentation consultants do?

BBMP khata documentation consultants collect your records, check them against each other, tell you what is missing and prepare the full set before the application is submitted. They also follow up with the BBMP, which is now part of the Greater Bengaluru Authority, if queries are raised.

  • Check the names, property number, dimensions and tax records across the deed, khata and tax receipts.
  • Find missing documents, such as an EC, a sketch or a registered division deed.
  • Prepare the document set in the format the application needs.
  • Explain whether you need a new khata, a transfer, a bifurcation or a correction.
  • Follow the application and answer queries.

5. Mutation and bifurcation records in plain words

Khata mutation

Mutation records a change of owner in the khata after a sale, inheritance or gift. It needs the deed that created the change and the usual khata and tax records. You can generally track the status on the BBMP e-Aasthi portal using your application number. Portal routes have been changing, so use the official portal for the current process.

Khata bifurcation

Bifurcation splits one khata into separate khatas after a property has been legally divided. A private family agreement is generally not enough. A registered deed, such as a partition or release deed, is the usual basis. Our page on khata bifurcation in Bangalore explains the process and the documents in detail.

See: khata bifurcation in Bangalore and our khata transfer guide.

6. How your documents are checked

Consultants check that the documents tell the same story. These checks catch most problems before the application is filed:

  1. The owner's name is the same on the deed, the khata, the tax receipt and the ID.
  2. The property number, address and dimensions match across the records.
  3. The title chain from the earlier deeds to the current deed is complete.
  4. The EC covers the right period and the right property.
  5. Property tax is paid up to date.
  6. The khata is A or B, and any claim of 'A-Khata in process' is backed by papers.
  7. For flats, the building has its approvals and the unit has its own record.

7. Common document problems

  • Owner names that differ across the deed, khata and ID.
  • A missing or unregistered deed for a divided property.
  • A missing sketch, or a sketch that does not show each portion.
  • Property tax unpaid or recorded under the wrong name.
  • A B-Khata that is described as 'A-Khata in process'.
  • Flats without a bifurcated khata or e-Khata.
  • Incomplete heir documents for an inherited property.
  • Using the wrong application route for the property's location.

Most of these can be found before you apply. That is the main value of a document check.

8. Can you prepare the documents yourself?

Often yes. If your records are clear, the names match and tax is paid, many owners complete a simple application themselves. Read the process first. Our guides explain it, including how to apply for e-Khata online and E-Aasthi and A and B khata explained.

A consultant helps most when:

  • The records do not match, or documents are missing.
  • The property is inherited, divided or jointly owned.
  • You are an NRI or cannot visit the offices.
  • A previous application was returned.
  • You are a developer with many units.

If you live abroad, see our NRI services. For problems with existing records, see fixing a property problem.

9. How to choose a khata documentation consultant

  • Will you check my documents before you quote?
  • What is included: document check, preparation, filing, follow-up?
  • What are the government fees and your professional fee? Are they shown separately, in writing?
  • Who is my contact, and how will I be updated?
  • What happens if the application is returned?

Be careful of anyone who guarantees approval or a fixed number of days, or asks for payment without a written scope. The authority decides every application.

Frequently asked questions

What is khata documentation?

Khata documentation is the set of records needed to create, update, transfer or split a khata. Commonly it includes the sale deed or title deed, the existing khata, property tax receipts, an encumbrance certificate, a property sketch and owner identity proof. Mutation and bifurcation cases need extra records.

Who are BBMP khata documentation consultants?

They are consultants who check your documents against each other, find what is missing, prepare the application set and follow it up with the BBMP, which is now part of the Greater Bengaluru Authority. Marvel Consultants offers this for Bengaluru property owners, buyers, NRIs and developers.

What documents are needed for a khata application in Bangalore?

Commonly the registered sale or title deed, the encumbrance certificate, the latest property tax receipt, owner identity proof and, where relevant, the approved plan or layout and property photographs. The exact list depends on the case, so confirm the current official checklist before you apply.

What is khata mutation?

Mutation is the process of recording a change of owner in the khata after a sale, inheritance, gift or similar event, so that the khata and property tax record show the new owner. It needs the deed that created the change, plus the usual khata and tax documents.

How do I check my e-Khata mutation status?

Application status is generally tracked on the BBMP e-Aasthi portal using your application number or property ID. Portal names and routes have been changing, so check the official portal for the current route, or ask us to track it.

What is khata bifurcation and what documents does it need?

Bifurcation splits one khata into separate khatas after a property has been legally divided. It generally needs the registered deed that supports the division, the existing khata, tax receipts, a sketch showing each portion and owner identity proof. See our khata bifurcation page for details.

Can I prepare the documents myself?

Often yes, for a simple case with clear records. Many owners complete simple applications themselves. A consultant helps most when the records do not match, documents are missing, the property is inherited or divided, or you cannot visit the offices.

What are the most common document problems?

Owner names that differ across the deed, khata and ID; missing or unregistered division documents; unpaid tax; a B-Khata described as 'A-Khata in process'; and flats without their own khata. Most can be found by checking the documents against each other before you apply.
