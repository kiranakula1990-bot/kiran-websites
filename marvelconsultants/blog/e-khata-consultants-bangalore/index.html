Property owners in Bengaluru increasingly deal with digital property records, online applications and document checks. The e-Aasthi portal provides an online route for relevant khata services, but completing an application can still involve understanding the property's existing records, identifying the correct service and making sure the information submitted is consistent.

This is where an e-Khata consultant can help. A consultant does not replace the authority or decide whether a property qualifies for a particular khata. The role is to help the owner understand the records, prepare the application correctly, submit the relevant information and follow up on the application.

1. What is an e-Khata, and who are e-Khata consultants?

An e-Khata is an electronic form of a property's khata record maintained through the relevant local authority's digital system. The record contains property and owner details used for municipal administration and property-tax purposes. The exact services and records available depend on the property's jurisdiction and the applicable portal process.

An e-Khata should not be treated as a substitute for a registered title deed. It is a civic property record; ownership and title questions require examination of the relevant legal documents. A property's khata category, approvals, location and history can all affect which application route is appropriate.

E-Khata consultants assist owners with the practical work around these records. This may include reviewing the existing khata, checking the sale deed and tax details, identifying missing information, preparing digital documents, assisting with the application and monitoring its progress.

In Bengaluru, it is particularly important to establish which authority and portal route applies to the property. A property within one jurisdiction may not follow the same process as a property in another local body. A consultant should check the property's location and existing records before advising on the application.

Not every property owner needs professional assistance. If your documents are complete, the property details match across the records and you are comfortable using the official portal, you may be able to handle a straightforward application yourself.

Professional help can be useful when the records are unclear, the application has been returned, or you are unsure which service applies to your property. It can also help when you are managing a property from outside Bengaluru or handling several properties at once.

4. Draft e-Khata and Final e-Khata

Property owners may encounter the terms Draft e-Khata and Final e-Khata while checking their records or application progress. They should not assume that the two terms describe interchangeable documents or that seeing a draft automatically means every remaining requirement has been completed.

Draft e-Khata

A draft record is an interim version made available for checking or verification in the applicable process. The owner should carefully compare the displayed information with the supporting documents. Check the spelling of the owner's name, property identification details, address, measurements and other fields shown in the draft.

Final e-Khata

A final record is the completed electronic khata record issued through the applicable authority process. The owner should review the final details and retain the available copy for future reference. If an error is noticed, the owner should check the current official correction procedure rather than assuming that a draft-stage change can still be made in the same way.

The exact screens, terminology and steps on the portal may change. Always rely on the current official portal instructions for the status and requirements applicable to your property.

5. How an e-Khata application works

Although the exact route depends on the property and the service selected, a careful application generally involves the following stages:

  1. Identify the property and jurisdiction. Confirm the property's location, existing khata details and the authority responsible for the record.
  2. Understand the requirement. Establish whether the owner needs a new e-Khata, a transfer, a correction, conversion guidance or help with an existing application.
  3. Collect the supporting documents. Gather the registered deed, existing khata details, property-tax records, identity documents and any additional records relevant to the case.
  4. Compare the information. Check names, property identifiers, address, measurements and other details across the available records.
  5. Resolve gaps where possible. Identify missing documents or inconsistencies and understand what supporting evidence or correction may be required.
  6. Prepare the application. Follow the current official portal instructions and provide the information and documents requested for the selected service.
  7. Retain the acknowledgement. Keep the application reference and copies of the submitted information so that the application can be followed up.
  8. Respond to queries. If the authority raises a query or returns the application, understand the reason and provide the relevant clarification or documents through the applicable route.
  9. Review the resulting record. When the record is made available, check the details and retain the electronic copy.

These are general stages, not a promise that every application follows the same sequence. The official portal and the concerned authority determine the applicable process. A consultant can help organise the work, but cannot guarantee an approval or override an authority's decision.

6. Common e-Khata problems consultants help address

Many difficulties begin with a mismatch between the information in the portal and the documents held by the owner. The right response depends on the exact error and the source of the information.

Correction problems

  • The owner's name is spelt differently in the deed and the existing property record.
  • The property number, address or other identifying detail appears incorrect.
  • The portal entry does not match the supporting tax or ownership documents.
  • The owner is unsure which document should support a requested correction.

A consultant can compare the records and help identify the discrepancy. The owner may need to obtain or correct a supporting record before the portal entry can be changed.

Download problems

  • The owner cannot locate the relevant property record on the portal.
  • The available record is not the document the owner expected.
  • The owner is unsure whether the displayed record is a draft or a final record.
  • The owner needs help understanding the information shown in the downloaded document.

Before attempting repeated downloads, confirm the property identifiers and the portal route. Keep the application reference and any available acknowledgement. Use the official portal for the current download instructions.

Status problems

  • An application appears pending and the owner does not know what stage it has reached.
  • A query or return message is difficult to understand.
  • The owner has submitted documents but is unsure whether further information is required.
  • The application details are unavailable or do not appear as expected.

Keep the application number and the exact message displayed. A consultant can help review the available information and identify a possible follow-up action. Only the concerned authority can confirm the official application status and decide the outcome.

7. Who uses e-Khata consultants?

Different property owners seek assistance for different reasons. The service required depends on the property record and the owner's objective.

  • Homeowners: Owners who need help understanding their existing khata, applying for an e-Khata or checking whether the record needs correction.
  • Recent property buyers: Buyers who need to understand the documents and steps involved in updating the civic record after a purchase.
  • Families handling inherited property: Families who need to review the existing record and the documents supporting a change following inheritance.
  • NRI property owners: Owners living outside India who need help coordinating property documents and understanding the application process.
  • Apartment owners: Owners who need to understand how their unit's record relates to the building and project documents.
  • Developers and property managers: People coordinating records or applications for multiple properties or units.

For inherited, jointly owned or divided properties, the ownership documents may need careful review before an application is selected. For apartments and larger developments, the project's approvals and the status of individual unit records may also matter.

8. How to choose an e-Khata consultant

Choose a consultant who explains the work clearly and does not promise an outcome that is controlled by the authority. Before engaging anyone, ask practical questions about the scope and handling of your documents.

  • Will you review my existing khata and supporting documents before recommending a service?
  • Will you explain which application route appears relevant to my property?
  • Does your scope include document checking, application preparation, filing assistance and follow-up?
  • What information or documents do you need from me?
  • How will you share updates and explain any query raised on the application?
  • Will government charges and professional service charges be explained separately?
  • How will my personal and property documents be handled?

Be cautious of anyone guaranteeing approval, promising a fixed outcome or claiming to influence an authority's decision. Ask for the agreed scope in writing and keep your own copies of every document and acknowledgement. Do not share portal passwords or one-time passwords unless the official process specifically requires an action that you understand and control.

Frequently asked questions

1. What do e-Khata consultants do?

E-Khata consultants help property owners understand their existing records, check supporting documents, identify missing information, prepare application documents and follow up on an e-Khata application. The exact assistance depends on the property and the service required.

2. Do I need a consultant to apply for e-Khata in Bangalore?

Not always. Owners with complete and consistent records who are comfortable using the official portal may be able to apply themselves. A consultant may help when documents are missing, details do not match, the property has a complicated history or an earlier application has been returned.

3. What documents are generally needed for an e-Khata application?

The documents depend on the property and application type. They may include the registered sale deed, existing khata details, property-tax records, owner identity proof and other supporting property records. Check the current official checklist for your specific application.

4. What is the difference between Draft e-Khata and Final e-Khata?

A draft is an interim record made available for checking or verification in the applicable process. A final e-Khata is the completed electronic record issued through the authority's process. Review the details carefully and follow the current official instructions for any correction.

5. Can an e-Khata consultant correct my owner's name or property details?

A consultant can compare the portal information with your supporting documents, identify the discrepancy and help you understand the relevant correction route. The correction is subject to the applicable rules and the concerned authority's decision.

6. Can a consultant help with B-Khata to A-Khata conversion?

A consultant can review the available property records and help you understand the relevant eligibility and documentation requirements for exploring conversion. Whether a property qualifies and whether conversion is approved are decisions for the concerned authority.

7. Can I get help if my e-Khata application is pending?

Yes. A consultant can review the application reference and the information available to help identify a possible follow-up action. Keep any acknowledgement or query message. The official status and decision must be confirmed by the concerned authority.

8. Can NRIs use e-Khata consultants in Bangalore?

NRIs can seek assistance with coordinating property documents and understanding the application process while living outside Bengaluru. The documents, authorisations and any in-person requirements depend on the specific case and the current official process.

9. Can a consultant guarantee e-Khata approval?

No consultant should guarantee approval. The concerned authority examines the property records and application and makes the decision. A consultant can help prepare and follow up on the application but cannot control the outcome.
