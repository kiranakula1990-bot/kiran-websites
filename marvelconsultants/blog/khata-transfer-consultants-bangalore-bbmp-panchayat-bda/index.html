Most khata transfers go wrong for a simple reason: the owner applied to the wrong authority, or the papers did not match. This guide explains what a khata transfer is, how the process differs across BBMP, Panchayat and BDA jurisdictions, when a consultant is worth hiring, and how to apply, including online.

1. What is a khata transfer?

A khata transfer, also called mutation, is the process of changing the owner's name on a property's khata record. It is needed after any change of ownership: a sale, a gift, an inheritance, a partition or a release deed.

It is not the same as registration. Registration at the sub-registrar office records the deed. The khata transfer updates the civic record, so that the khata and the property tax account show the new owner. A khata is a record used for property tax and municipal purposes. It is not proof of title by itself.

Why it matters: banks, builders and buyers usually want to see the khata in the current owner's name. Without a transfer, tax receipts and utility records may continue in the previous owner's name, and a later sale, loan or plan approval can be delayed.

2. What does a khata transfer consultant do?

A khata transfer consultant prepares and follows your application so that it is complete and consistent when it reaches the authority. In practice, the work covers six steps:

  1. Check the existing khata, tax record and encumbrance certificate against your deed.
  2. Identify which authority holds the record: BBMP, a Gram Panchayat or the BDA.
  3. Find mismatches and missing documents, and fix them before filing where possible.
  4. Prepare the application and the supporting documents for your type of transfer.
  5. File the application, answer queries and attend verification where needed.
  6. Follow the file to the new khata, then update the tax account and e-Khata where relevant.

A consultant cannot decide the result. The authority decides every application. A good consultant tells you what is needed, in plain language, and does not promise approval or a fixed number of days.

5. BBMP khata transfer consultants

BBMP khata transfer consultants handle transfers for properties whose record sits with the BBMP, now part of the Greater Bengaluru Authority. This is the most common case for sites, houses and flats inside the city.

A BBMP khata transfer generally needs the registered deed, the existing khata certificate and extract, the latest tax-paid receipt, an encumbrance certificate and your identity proof. The ward or zonal office checks that the name, property number and dimensions match across these records. Most returned applications come from a mismatch, such as an initial missing, a site number written differently, or a built-up area that differs from the tax record.

After the transfer, the tax account and the e-Khata record usually need to follow, so a consultant normally handles those too.

Read the detailed process in our BBMP khata transfer guide. For the related e-Khata steps, see how to apply for e-Khata online. If your khata is a B-Khata, see B-Khata to A-Khata conversion.

6. Panchayat khata transfer consultants

Panchayat khata transfer consultants handle transfers for properties whose record is with a Gram Panchayat. This applies to properties in village limits and to some areas on the edge of the city.

A Panchayat transfer usually leans more on land records. Alongside the deed and tax receipts, you may be asked for the RTC (record of rights), an encumbrance certificate, an affidavit and the previous owner's khata extract. The Panchayat office may also verify the property on site. Practices can differ between Panchayats, so confirm what your local office asks for.

Many owners also ask whether a Panchayat khata is the same as an e-Khata. It is not, and the difference affects what you can do next. Read Panchayat khata vs e-Khata and our Panchayat khata guide.

7. BDA khata transfer consultants, and how to apply online

BDA khata transfer consultants handle transfers for properties in BDA layouts or allotted by the BDA. Here the records can be split. The BDA may hold the allotment or possession papers, while the civic khata is handled separately. A consultant checks both sets before you apply, so that one record does not contradict the other.

Read the detailed process in our BDA khata transfer guide.

Can you apply for a khata transfer online?

Sometimes. Online routes exist for some properties and some services in Bangalore, and they have been changing. Whether you can apply online depends on the authority and the type of transfer. A general approach:

  1. Confirm the authority named in your khata, tax receipt and deed.
  2. Check the official portal for that authority to see whether your transfer type is offered online.
  3. Scan your documents clearly and check the names and property details match.
  4. Fill in the online application, upload the documents and keep the acknowledgement or application number.
  5. Track the application using that number, and respond to any query promptly.
  6. If your property is not offered online, apply through the office the authority names.

This applies to BBMP, Gram Panchayat and BDA properties alike: the portal and the route may differ for each, so check before you start. If you want us to confirm the route and prepare the application, send us your documents.

9. Common khata transfer problems

  • Name spelt differently in the deed, khata, tax record and ID.
  • Property number or dimensions that differ between the deed and the khata.
  • Tax dues in the previous owner's name.
  • A gift or release deed that was never registered.
  • Heirs who have not signed a release, or a will that needs further legal steps.
  • The wrong authority approached because the record sits elsewhere.
  • A flat whose parent khata was never divided into units.

Most of these can be found before you apply. That check is the main value of a consultant.

Frequently asked questions

What is a khata transfer?

A khata transfer, also called mutation, changes the owner's name on a property's khata record after a sale, gift, inheritance, partition or release deed. Registration at the sub-registrar office records the deed. The khata transfer updates who is shown as owner and who pays the property tax.

What does a khata transfer consultant do?

A consultant checks your documents against the khata and tax records, finds mismatches, prepares the application, files it with the right authority, answers queries and follows the file to the new khata. They do not decide the outcome. The authority does.

Is it worth hiring a khata transfer consultant, or can I do it myself?

For a simple case with matching records and tax paid, many owners can apply themselves. A consultant helps most when names or property details do not match, documents are missing, the property is inherited or jointly owned, you live abroad, or an earlier application was returned.

How do I know whether my property is under BBMP, a Panchayat or the BDA?

Check the authority named on your existing khata, tax receipt and sale deed. Many areas have changed hands between authorities over time, so do not assume from the address alone. If the documents are unclear, a consultant can confirm the authority before you apply.

Can I apply for a khata transfer online in Bangalore?

Online routes exist for some properties and some services, and they have been changing. Check the official portal for the current process for your property. Panchayat and BDA properties may follow a different route from BBMP properties, so confirm which applies to yours.

How do I apply for a khata transfer for a Gram Panchayat property in Karnataka?

Panchayat khata records are usually handled through the Gram Panchayat office, and some services are offered online. Land records such as the RTC, the registered deed and tax receipts are commonly asked for. Confirm the current route with the Panchayat or ask a consultant.

Is a BDA khata transfer different from a BBMP khata transfer?

Yes, it can be. BDA-allotted sites may involve BDA records and, in some cases, BDA-specific documents that a BBMP transfer does not need. The documents and route depend on the property, so confirm with the authority named in your records.

Which documents are usually needed for a khata transfer?

Commonly the registered deed, the existing khata certificate and extract, the latest property tax receipt, an encumbrance certificate and the applicant's identity proof. Inheritance cases add death and legal-heir documents. The exact list depends on the property and the authority.

How do I transfer a khata after the owner's death, a gift or a release deed?

Each case needs the registered or supporting document that created the change, such as legal-heir papers, a registered gift deed or a registered release deed, along with the usual khata and tax records. Read the detailed guide for your case before applying.
