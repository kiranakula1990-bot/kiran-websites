Vinyl Floor Polishing and Maintenance Guide: When to Repolish

Enquiry Form
Enter a 10-digit mobile number

25 November 2026 | EFCS Blog

Vinyl floors lose their shine gradually. Dullness, visible scratch patterns and a sticky feel underfoot are the usual signs it is time to repolish. How often depends mainly on foot traffic, and the EFCS vinyl page suggests every 6 to 12 months. Here is how to tell, and what the process involves.

This guide is for homeowners, apartment owners and small business owners in Bangalore who want to know when vinyl needs professional attention and what they can do themselves. To see the service, visit the EFCS vinyl floor polishing page.

1. Does your vinyl floor need repolishing? A four-point self-check

Look at the floor in daylight, and again with a lamp or a window light across it from the side. Raking light shows wear that you cannot see from above.

What to checkWhat you may noticeWhat it can mean
Dull, patchy shineWalkways look flatter or less even than areas under furnitureSurface wear from regular foot traffic
Scratch and scuff patternsLines and marks become clearer when light falls across the floorRepeated friction or movement of furniture and footwear
Sticky or tacky feelThe floor feels sticky again soon after moppingResidue, unsuitable cleaner or a surface-care issue that needs checking
Stains or hazy filmMarks or cloudiness remain after normal cleaningBuild-up or discolouration; try a suitable mild clean before assuming polishing is needed

If you can tick two or more, a professional inspection is worth booking. If you tick only the fourth, try one thorough clean with a mild cleaner first. A floor that has only residue on it may not need polishing.

2. When it is not a polish job: repair or replacement signs

Polishing works on the surface. It cannot fix damage to the structure of the floor. Call for an inspection if you see any of the signs below, because the answer may be a repair.

SignWhy polishing may not be enoughWhat to do
Cracks, tears or deep gougesThe floor covering is physically damagedAsk whether the affected area can be repaired or needs replacement
Curling edges or lifting seamsThe material may be losing adhesion or the joints may be failingHave the cause checked before any surface treatment
Bubbles, soft spots or movementThere may be an issue below the floor coveringArrange an inspection; do not try to polish it flat
Dark patches that returnMoisture, staining or another underlying issue may remainIdentify and address the cause first
Faded colour or worn-through patternThe decorative layer may be damaged beyond surface restorationAsk whether repair or replacement is the practical option

The EFCS vinyl page says restoration is a cost-effective alternative to replacement for floors with wear, scratches or discolouration, and that it makes replacement unnecessary in most cases. An inspection tells you which case you have.

3. How often should vinyl floors be polished?

There is no single answer, because foot traffic decides. A hallway or shop floor wears faster than a spare bedroom. The EFCS vinyl page suggests professional polishing every 6 to 12 months, depending on foot traffic and wear.

Area or conditionTypical wear patternWhat to watch for
Entrances, corridors and kitchensFrequent walking, dirt and repeated cleaningCheck for dull paths, scuffs and residue more often
Shop floorsRegular customer and staff movementMonitor high-traffic routes and entrance areas
Living and dining areasModerate daily use and furniture movementLook for uneven shine and scratches around furniture
Bedrooms and low-use roomsLess frequent foot trafficInspect periodically rather than following a rigid schedule
Floors cleaned with harsh productsSurface finish may be affected by unsuitable chemicalsCheck the care guide and change to a suitable cleaner

Do not wait for the whole floor to look bad. Repolishing while the wear is still on the surface is simpler than restoring a floor that has been worn through.

4. Check your vinyl type before you polish

Vinyl is not one product. It comes as sheet, tile and plank, and the surface finish differs between makers. Some floors have a factory-applied wear layer, and some makers advise against adding polish, wax or sealer to certain products.

Vinyl typeWhat to checkBefore arranging work
Vinyl sheetProduct finish, seams and manufacturer's cleaning instructionsCheck whether the maker permits polishing or any added coating
Vinyl tilesTile condition, joints and any loose or damaged sectionsIdentify damaged tiles and confirm the recommended care method
Vinyl planksWear layer, locking or glued joints and product-specific instructionsCheck whether polish, wax or sealer is permitted for that product
Unknown product or older floorAny available receipt, product name or care guideShare clear photos and let EFCS inspect before deciding on treatment

If you still have the care guide or the receipt, keep it handy. If you do not, send EFCS clear photos and the name of the product if you know it. EFCS inspects the floor first and assesses the wear, so the work suits the floor you have.

5. What a professional vinyl repolish involves

The EFCS vinyl page describes five stages. Here is what each means for you.

StageWhat happensWhat it checks or achieves
InspectionThe floor type, condition, wear and problem areas are assessedHelps decide whether polishing is suitable
Deep cleaningSuitable cleaning removes dirt and surface build-upPrepares the floor for the next stage
Polishing or buffingThe appropriate method is selected for the floor's condition and finishAddresses suitable surface dullness and wear
Sealing or protectionOnly considered where the product and maker's guidance allow itAny finish must be compatible with the vinyl
Final checkThe treated area is reviewed and care instructions are discussedConfirms the result and the next steps for use and maintenance

The EFCS vinyl page says the process typically takes a few hours to a full day, depending on the size of the area and the extent of the cleaning and restoration needed.

How to prepare for the visit

  • Clear small items, rugs and loose objects from the floor.
  • Point out stains, soft spots, lifted seams and places that worry you.
  • Tell EFCS the type of vinyl, if you know it.
  • Ask when the floor can be walked on and when furniture can go back, and follow that advice.

6. How to care for vinyl between professional visits

Good daily care keeps a polish looking good for longer. Most of it takes a few minutes.

WhenCare routine
Every day or twoSweep or vacuum regularly, using a vacuum without a hard beater bar
WeeklyDamp mop with a mild cleaner and a well-wrung mop; avoid excess water around seams
Immediately after spillsWipe them up promptly and follow the flooring maker's cleaning guidance
Every dayUse entrance mats, fit felt pads under furniture legs and lift heavy items rather than dragging them
Every few monthsCheck high-traffic areas for dullness, scratches, sticky patches and lifting edges

Cleaning products: what to use and what to avoid

  • Use a mild, pH-neutral floor cleaner that suits vinyl, diluted as the label says.
  • Check your flooring maker's care guide before you buy a new product.
  • Avoid abrasive powders, scouring pads and strong solvents.
  • Avoid oil-based soaps, which can leave a film.
  • Do not add wax or polish unless the maker says the floor is suitable.
  • Avoid steam mops unless the maker allows them.

Frequently asked questions

What are the signs that my vinyl floor needs repolishing?

The usual signs are a dull, patchy look in the places people walk most, scratch and scuff patterns that show when light falls across the floor, a sticky or tacky feel that returns soon after mopping, and stains or a hazy film that normal cleaning no longer removes. If you see cracks, tears, curling edges or lifting seams, that is damage rather than wear, and the floor needs repair or replacement instead of polish.

Is repolishing vinyl cheaper than replacing it?

The EFCS vinyl page describes restoration as a cost-effective alternative to replacement for floors with wear, scratches or discolouration, and says it makes replacement unnecessary in most cases. The cost depends on the area, the condition of the floor and the type of vinyl, so EFCS quotes after an inspection and no fixed rate is published here. When you compare, ask for a replacement quote that includes removing the old floor and preparing the base.

How can I care for vinyl floors between professional visits?

Sweep or vacuum regularly, using a vacuum without a hard beater bar. Damp mop with a mild cleaner and a well-wrung mop, because too much water can seep into seams. Wipe up spills quickly. Put mats at entrances, fit felt pads under furniture legs, and lift heavy items rather than dragging them. Keep strong sunlight and hot items off the floor where you can.

What are the best cleaning products for vinyl floors at home?

Use a mild, pH-neutral floor cleaner that suits vinyl, diluted as the label says, and check your flooring maker's care guide first. Avoid abrasive powders, scouring pads, strong solvents and oil-based soaps, which can scratch the surface or leave a film. Do not add wax or polish to a floor unless the maker says it is suitable, and avoid steam mops unless the maker allows them.

How long does a vinyl polish job last?

It depends on foot traffic, how the floor is cleaned and the finish applied. Busy areas such as entrances, kitchens and corridors dull first. The EFCS vinyl page suggests professional polishing every 6 to 12 months depending on foot traffic and wear. Gentle daily cleaning helps a polish last, and harsh cleaners shorten it.
