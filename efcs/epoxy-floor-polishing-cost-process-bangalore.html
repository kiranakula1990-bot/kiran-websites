1. Epoxy coating or epoxy polishing: what is the difference?

People use the two terms together, but they are different jobs, and the difference changes your quote.

JobWhat it isWhen you need it
Epoxy coating (new epoxy floor)A resin layer is applied over a prepared floor. It forms a seamless, protective surface.A new or worn floor, such as bare concrete, that needs a durable finish.
Epoxy polishing (gloss restoration)An existing epoxy floor is worked to remove surface marks and bring back the shine.An epoxy floor that has gone dull from traffic and cleaning.
BothPrepare, coat, let it cure, then polish and finish.A new floor where a high-gloss finish is wanted.

When you ask for a quote, say which of the three you need. A quote for coating only and a quote for coating and polishing are not comparable.

3. Commercial or industrial grade: which does your site need?

The grade is chosen by what the floor must carry and resist. This is a general guide, not a specification. EFCS recommends a system after the site check.

Commercial gradeIndustrial grade
Typical sitesOffices, showrooms, retail, light storage, corridors, car parks.Warehouses, factories, workshops, loading bays, cold or wet process areas.

Mixed use is common, for example an office floor next to a warehouse. Say so in your enquiry. If your floor is plain concrete and you are choosing between epoxy and polished concrete, see concrete floor polishing.

4. How is an epoxy floor applied? The process, step by step

Preparation decides how long an epoxy floor lasts. These are the steps, in order.

  1. Site check and consultation. The team looks at the floor, its use, the area and any moisture, oil or cracks, and recommends a system.
  2. Surface preparation. The floor is cleaned and, where needed, ground so the epoxy can bond. Cracks and holes are repaired. Ask how moisture in concrete is checked.
  3. Primer and epoxy application. The resin is mixed to the maker's instructions and applied in an even layer, with extra coats where the system needs them.
  4. Curing. The floor is left undisturbed while the epoxy hardens. This is the stage that decides when you can use the floor again (see Section 5).
  5. Polishing and finishing. After the epoxy has cured, the surface is polished to improve the gloss and smoothness.
  6. Final inspection and care guidance. The team checks the finish and gives guidance on cleaning and care.

EFCS describes the same stages on its epoxy floor polishing page. This guide adds the detail and the planning points.

5. How long does epoxy flooring take to cure?

Cure time is the first question facility managers ask, because it decides when the area can reopen. Two different times matter: how long the job takes on site, and how long the floor needs before it carries load.

StageWhat it meansPlanning note
Job on siteThe EFCS epoxy page says a standard coating job typically takes 2 to 4 days, including curing. It depends on the floor size and condition.Larger or damaged floors can take longer. Ask for the timeline for your site in writing.
Light foot trafficMany epoxy systems allow careful walking after about a day.Keep trolleys, equipment and spills off the floor.
Full cure for heavy loads and chemicalsOften around a week, depending on the product, the room temperature and the thickness.Plan forklifts and heavy loads for after this stage.

These stages are general. The product data sheet and the applicator decide the real times for your floor, and cooler or damp conditions can slow curing. Always ask for the cure times of the exact system in writing.

How to plan around cure time

  • Work in zones or phases, so part of the site stays in use.
  • Schedule the job at night, on a weekend or during a shutdown, if your operations allow it.
  • Plan alternate routes for staff and equipment while an area cures.
  • Keep the heaviest traffic and equipment for the last zone to reopen.
  • Mark cured and uncured zones clearly with barriers and signs.

6. How long does an epoxy floor last, and what shortens its life?

The EFCS epoxy page says an epoxy floor can last 10 to 20 years, depending on traffic, maintenance and conditions. Treat that as the best case, not a promise. These shorten the life:

  • Poor preparation or moisture in the concrete.
  • Heavy impact, dragged pallets or sharp equipment.
  • Chemical spills beyond the system's resistance.
  • Harsh cleaners or abrasive pads.
  • Direct sunlight, which can yellow some epoxy types.

See the EFCS epoxy floor polishing page for the service details.

7. How do you maintain an epoxy floor?

  • Sweep or dust-mop regularly to remove grit.
  • Use a mild cleaner and avoid abrasive pads.
  • Wipe spills promptly, especially chemicals.
  • Use mats or protective pads where heavy equipment moves.
  • Inspect the floor and arrange repairs for chips or damaged areas.
  • Re-polish when the gloss fades, if the system and condition call for it.

EFCS lists inspection, cleaning and minor repairs as part of its epoxy maintenance service. For routine cleaning teams, see EFCS housekeeping services.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between epoxy floor polishing and regular floor polish?

Regular floor polish is applied to or worked into an existing surface such as stone, tile or concrete. Epoxy flooring is a resin coating that is applied to a prepared floor and forms a seamless layer. Polishing an epoxy floor restores or improves its gloss. Because the two are different jobs, ask for the scope in writing: coating, polishing, or both.

How long does an epoxy floor last?

It depends on the traffic, the grade of epoxy, how well the floor was prepared and how it is maintained. The EFCS epoxy page says an epoxy floor can last 10 to 20 years under the right conditions. Heavy forklift traffic, chemical spills and poor preparation can shorten that. Ask for the expected life of your system in writing.

How long before we can walk on a new epoxy floor?

It depends on the product. Many epoxy systems allow light foot traffic after about a day, but reaching full strength for heavy loads and chemicals usually takes longer, often around a week. The product data sheet and the applicator decide. EFCS says a standard coating job typically takes 2 to 4 days including curing, depending on floor size and condition. Confirm the timeline for your floor in writing.

How do I maintain an epoxy floor?

Sweep or dust-mop regularly, mop gently with a mild cleaner, wipe up chemical spills quickly, and avoid harsh chemicals and abrasive tools. Use mats or protective pads where heavy equipment moves. The EFCS epoxy page also lists routine inspections and minor repairs as part of its maintenance service.

How does the cost of epoxy compare with other finishes?

There is no single answer, because the price depends on the area, the floor condition, the system and the finish you choose. Epoxy adds a coating on top of the floor, while concrete polishing works the existing concrete. Compare quotes on the same scope, the same area and the same expected life, and ask what each option needs for maintenance.
