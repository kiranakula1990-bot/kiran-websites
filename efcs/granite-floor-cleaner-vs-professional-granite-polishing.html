Granite Floor Cleaner vs Professional Granite Polishing: What Actually Works

Enter a 10-digit mobile number

By EFCS | Last reviewed: 27 October 2026 | Reading time: about 8 minutes

A pH-neutral granite cleaner is the right choice for daily care, but no cleaner can bring back shine that has worn away. Once granite stays dull after a proper clean, the surface is worn and needs professional honing, polishing and sealing.

This guide explains which cleaners to use, which to avoid and when to call a granite polishing service in Bangalore.

Granite is durable, but daily grit, spills, unsuitable cleaners and worn sealers can affect its appearance. Cleaning and polishing solve different problems. Start by checking whether the floor is dirty or whether its surface has lost its finish.

1. What to use on granite floors every day

Use a good-quality pH-neutral granite cleaner suitable for natural stone, diluted according to its label. Sweep or dust-mop first so grit does not scratch the surface, then damp-mop and remove excess moisture with a clean mop.

  • Use clean water and a soft microfibre mop for routine care.
  • Wipe spills promptly, especially coloured liquids and oily substances.
  • Use mats at entrances to reduce grit tracked indoors.
  • Test a new product on a small, hidden area before general use.

Check the product label before using any cleaner. If you are unsure whether a product is suitable, ask a stone-care professional before applying it across the floor.

2. What damages granite (avoid these)

  • Vinegar and lemon: these are acidic and may damage the sealer or dull some stones sold as granite, particularly those containing calcite.
  • Descalers and acidic bathroom cleaners: do not use them on granite floors unless a stone-care professional confirms the product is suitable.
  • Repeated bleach or ammonia use: these can affect sealers, finishes or certain stones. Never mix bleach with ammonia or acids.
  • Abrasive powders and rough pads: these may scratch or mark the finish.
  • Wax- or oil-based shine products: these can leave a film that attracts dirt or yellows over time.
  • Ceramic-tile-only cleaners: do not assume they are safe for natural stone.

Important: granite is generally more acid-resistant than marble, but natural stone composition varies. Some stones sold as granite contain acid-sensitive minerals. Use products specifically suitable for your stone.

If the correct cleaner no longer restores the appearance, a granite polishing service in Bangalore can assess whether the surface needs restoration.

3. The water-drop test: is your sealer still working?

  1. Choose a clean, dry and inconspicuous spot on the granite.
  2. Place a few drops of water on the surface.
  3. Watch the area for a few minutes. If the stone darkens as it absorbs water, the sealer may be worn or the stone may be absorbent.
  4. If the water beads and stays on top, the sealer may still be working at that spot.
  5. Repeat in two or three places, including a busy walkway.

This is a simple screening test, not a definitive assessment of the sealer. Stone porosity and previous treatments affect the result. Ask a professional if you are unsure before resealing.

4. Signs your granite needs professional polishing

  • The floor stays dull after a proper clean with a suitable product.
  • Scratches or worn traffic paths remain visible when the floor is clean.
  • The finish looks patchy or uneven in different areas.
  • Old coatings or polish leave a persistent film that routine cleaning cannot remove.
  • Water absorption varies noticeably between areas and needs professional assessment.

Cleaning removes dirt and residue. It does not repair a worn stone surface. If the floor remains dull, ask a granite polishing service in Bangalore to inspect it before choosing a treatment.

5. What a professional granite polishing service does

The right process depends on the stone, its thickness, existing finish and the condition of the floor. A professional assessment may include these stages:

  1. Deep clean: remove soil, grease and unsuitable coatings or residue where appropriate.
  2. Hone or grind if needed: use suitable diamond abrasives to address surface wear and scratches. Honing removes a thin surface layer, so it should only be done when necessary.
  3. Polish: use progressively finer diamond abrasives or an appropriate stone-polishing method to achieve the intended finish.
  4. Seal if suitable: apply a compatible penetrating sealer where the stone and its condition call for it, following product instructions.

Not every floor needs every stage. Over-polishing can remove more stone than necessary, and the achievable result depends on the stone and existing damage.

6. Store-bought granite polish: what it can and cannot do

Some retail products add temporary gloss or leave a surface film. They may improve appearance briefly, but they do not remove scratches or restore a worn stone surface. Some films attract dirt or yellow as they age.

Real surface restoration may require professional machines and suitable abrasives. Choose a product based on its label and the stone type, and test it in an inconspicuous spot first.

7. Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using vinegar, lemon juice or acidic descalers on granite.
  • Assuming a stronger cleaner will work better than a stone-safe product.
  • Using abrasive pads or powders that can mark the finish.
  • Applying wax or shine products without checking whether they are suitable.
  • Sealing on a fixed calendar without checking the floor's condition.
  • Expecting cleaning to remove scratches or restore worn gloss.
  • Attempting grinding or honing without confirming the stone and its thickness.

Frequently asked questions

Which is the best granite floor cleaner?

Any good-quality pH-neutral stone cleaner, diluted as the label says. Avoid products labelled acidic, descaler or 'for ceramic tiles only', because they can strip the sealer and dull some stones.

Can I polish granite myself?

Store-bought granite polish adds a temporary shine but does not remove wear or scratches. Restoring real shine needs diamond abrasives and machines, which is why it is a professional job.

How often should granite floors be sealed?

Usually every one to three years, depending on the stone and the traffic. Use the water-drop test to check, rather than sealing by the calendar.

Is vinegar safe on granite?

No. Vinegar and lemon are acids. They can strip the sealer and can dull granites that contain calcite. Use a pH-neutral stone cleaner instead.

How do I do the water-drop test on granite?

Put a few drops of water on a clean, dry spot and watch it. If the stone darkens within a few minutes, it is absorbing water and the sealer has worn off. If the water beads and stays on top, the sealer is still working. Test two or three spots, including high-traffic areas.

Why is my granite still dull after cleaning?

If a proper clean does not bring the shine back, the surface itself is worn or scratched. Cleaning removes dirt, grease and residue, but it cannot restore gloss. At that point honing, polishing and sealing by a granite polishing service is the fix.

How often should granite be cleaned and polished professionally?

As a rough guide, a professional clean every three to six months suits busy floors, and polishing every one to three years suits worn floors. These are guides, not rules. The condition of the floor matters more than the calendar.
