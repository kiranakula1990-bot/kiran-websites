4. Italian, Makrana and Other Marble: Does the Type Change the Price?

Yes. Marble varieties can respond differently to grinding, honing, polishing and sealing. The name given to a stone is a useful starting point, but the actual material and its condition should be checked before choosing a method.

Marble typeWhat should be checkedQuoting consideration

If you are unsure which marble you have, send a clear photo and any available installation or purchase details. The team can advise whether an in-person assessment is required.

5. Mirror Finish vs Matte (Honed) Finish

A mirror finish is glossy and reflective. It can make the stone look brighter, but it also makes reflections and some surface marks more noticeable. A matte or honed finish has lower reflection and a softer appearance.

The right choice depends on your preferred look, the marble itself and how the room is used. Tell the team which finish you prefer when requesting a quotation. If you are undecided, ask to discuss suitable finish options after the floor has been assessed.

6. What Adds Cost, and What Can Keep It Down?

Work that may add to the scope

  • Deep scratches, etching or visibly uneven areas that need additional preparation.
  • Stains or damage that require separate assessment or treatment.
  • Many edges, corners, steps or difficult-to-reach areas.
  • Furniture movement, restricted access or special site arrangements.
  • Additional repairs or sealing that are not part of basic polishing.

Details that help the team quote accurately

  • Share clear photos in natural light, including close-ups of damaged areas.
  • Give the approximate floor area and number of rooms.
  • Tell the team about previous polishing, coatings or cleaning chemicals used.
  • Explain the finish you want and any areas of particular concern.
  • Large open floors are generally easier to assess and plan than spaces with many small sections.

7. What Does the Marble Polishing Process Include?

The exact process depends on the marble and the condition of the floor. A professional scope may include:

  1. Inspection: Identify the stone, inspect wear and damage, and discuss the finish required.
  2. Grinding or honing, if needed: Prepare the surface using a method and abrasive sequence suitable for the stone.
  3. Polishing: Refine the surface towards the agreed finish.
  4. Sealing, if suitable and included: Apply an appropriate stone sealer where required by the material and agreed scope.

Before work begins, ask which stages are included, how edges and corners will be handled, what finish is expected and whether sealing is included in the quotation.

Frequently Asked Questions

What affects the cost of marble polishing per sq ft?

The marble type, existing scratches or etching, floor condition, finish required, total area, layout and access all affect the work involved. EFCS checks photos or the floor before giving a quote.

How often should marble floors be repolished?

There is no fixed interval for every marble floor. Foot traffic, cleaning methods, spills, wear and the finish you want all matter. Consider an assessment when the floor remains dull, scratched or uneven after routine cleaning.

Is a mirror finish or a matte finish better for marble?

A mirror finish gives marble a high-gloss reflective appearance. A matte or honed finish has less reflection and a softer appearance. The suitable choice depends on the look you prefer, the floor condition and how the space is used.

Can I polish marble myself?

Routine care can be done with a suitable pH-neutral stone cleaner, but grinding and polishing require the correct machines, abrasives and technique. Incorrect products or methods can etch or damage marble. Do not use acid on marble.

Does marble polishing come with a warranty?

Warranty coverage should not be assumed to be the same for every floor or job. Ask EFCS to confirm the work scope, finish expectations, exclusions and any applicable warranty terms in writing with the quotation.
